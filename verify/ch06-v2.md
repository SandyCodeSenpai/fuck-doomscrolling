# Chapter 6 verification (v2, rewritten cards)
Checked: 77 cards. Correct as-is: 63. Fixed: 14. Added: 0. Deleted: 0.
## Fixes
- ch06-008 [UNSUPPORTED] deeper said read-through "tends to appear as a feature of a database platform rather than a standalone cache". The book states no such tendency, only that the cache must know how to query the database. Reworded as an inference illustrated by CacheFront.
- ch06-015 [UNSUPPORTED] deeper said in-process copies "get no invalidations" as a flat fact. The book only says to use a short TTL (seconds). Softened to "usually get no invalidations", matching ch06-029.
- ch06-019 [MISLEADING] hook said the 4% figure "forced a redesign". Per the book (6.3 In industry), the redesign (batching through mcrouter) was driven by packet rates being too high, not by the 4% itself. Hook reworded. [UNSUPPORTED] deeper claimed the 18× gain "is what made commit-log invalidation practical at Meta's size". The book draws no such causal link. Removed. [MCQ distractor arguably true] "Cache servers drop deletes for keys they never stored" describes, in effect, what happens to a delete for an absent key. Replaced with a clearly wrong option ("queue deletes until the key is next read").
- ch06-020 [MISLEADING] deeper said per-user routing to the database after a write "is what Meta does across regions with remote markers". Remote markers are per key (a reader that sees the marker reads from the primary region), not per-user routing, and the book lists them separately. Reworded.
- ch06-032 [WRONG/overclaim] hook said "Memcached knows three commands". The book says memcached is a simple server "with get, set and delete". It does not say those are its only commands, and real memcached has more. Hook reworded to "little more than get, set and delete".
- ch06-034 [MISLEADING] title "a proxy handling 5 billion requests per second" reads like one proxy instance. mcrouter runs on every web server, and the book's figure is "close to 5 billion" in aggregate by 2014. Title changed to "mcrouter: a proxy layer near 5 billion requests/sec".
- ch06-040 [UNSUPPORTED] hook "Meta patched cache races for years" invents a timeline. The book only says TAO "later" took over many graph reads, and both papers date from 2013. Hook reworded.
- ch06-046 [overclaim] title "so caches must grow". The book says flatter popularity makes "larger caches more valuable". Changed to "so bigger caches pay".
- ch06-048 [UNSUPPORTED] deeper said CacheLib's measurements suggest a working set much larger than DRAM "is common". The book reports flatter-than-Zipf, fast-changing popularity, which makes larger caches more valuable. Rephrased to what the book says.
- ch06-056 [MCQ distractor arguably true] "Flux replays the binlog to overwrite old fills": Flux does "invalidate or update Redis", so this could be read as partly right. Replaced with a clearly wrong option.
- ch06-059 [UNSUPPORTED] body said "Clustering and persistence behave the same way in both" (Redis and Valkey). The book says only that Valkey keeps compatibility with the Redis protocol and cluster design. Persistence removed. The deeper claim that "clients, tools and cluster setups keep working without changes" was also narrowed. [MISLEADING] takeaway "its durability still makes it a cache" read as praise for its durability. Rewritten as "Weak durability limits what it should store."
- ch06-063 [MISLEADING] takeaway "Your fsync interval is your data-loss window" only holds for AOF. For RDB the snapshot interval sets the window, and the card covers both modes. Changed to "fsync or snapshot interval".
- ch06-068 [MISLEADING] deeper said "Each zone's copy serves its zone's reads. 2 million per second...", which implies each zone gets 2M of its own reads, but that contradicts a 2M total peak. The book sizes each zone copy for the full 2 million reads/sec. Reworded to say exactly that.
- ch06-069 [MISLEADING] deeper said the example's 800M-user tail is "the long tail the CacheLib paper found". It conflated the illustrative example with the paper's measurements. Reworded.
