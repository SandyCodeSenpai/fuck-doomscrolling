# Chapter 4 verification (v2, after WRITING_STANDARD v2 rewrite)
Checked: 70 cards. Correct as-is: 55. Fixed: 15. Added: 0. Deleted: 0.
## Fixes
- ch04-002 [MISLEADING] deeper said a block-level snapshot "can only ever be crash-consistent"; the book says freezing the filesystem or checkpointing first gives an application-consistent snapshot (section "Snapshots"). Now says it is only crash-consistent when taken without help from the layers above.
- ch04-005 [UNSUPPORTED] deeper said the wasted IOPS/throughput "is why" clouds introduced gp3/io2/Premium SSD v2/Hyperdisk; the book gives no motive. Reworded: newer types avoid that waste by provisioning separately.
- ch04-011 [MISLEADING] hook asked "What did it take to reach trillions a day?", but the body only gives the timeline and numbers, not the causes. Hook now asks how far EBS has come since 2008, which the body answers.
- ch04-019 [MISLEADING] title "proven in TLA+", takeaway "Prove the protocol" and deeper "a proof covers the spec"; the book says the team specified the protocol in TLA+, ran a model checker and tested with Jepsen. It does not say the protocol was proven. Changed to "model-checked" / "check the protocol formally".
- ch04-024 [MISLEADING] title "99.9% durable means losing volumes every single year" reads as if one volume is lost every year. The 5-10 losses/year figure is for a 5,000-volume fleet. Title now says "a big fleet loses volumes every year".
- ch04-028 [UNSUPPORTED] title "One Google disk fed 2,500 machines at 1.2 TB/s" and deeper "served from a single volume". The book says Hyperdisk ML attaches one volume to up to 2,500 instances and, separately, that "Google reported 2,500 nodes reading at 1.2 TB/s" (source: a Colossus blog post). It never says the 1.2 TB/s came from one disk. Title now reads "Hyperdisk ML: 2,500 nodes reading at 1.2 TB/s", and "single volume" is removed from deeper.
- ch04-029 [MCQ distractor arguably true] "Standard HDD" was offered as a wrong answer to "which type ties performance to size tier". The book's table says nothing about Standard HDD's model, and in reality Azure Standard HDD/SSD are size-tiered too. Replaced with Google Hyperdisk Balanced, which the book says provisions performance separately. Question and why updated.
- ch04-031 [MCQ distractor/why unsupported] "Incremental point-in-time snapshots" was a distractor, with why claiming it is "common to all three clouds". The book's convergence box doesn't list snapshots and never says Google or Azure snapshots are incremental. Replaced with "SSD-backed volume types", which the book does list as shared.
- ch04-035 [UNSUPPORTED] body opened with "Relational databases are usually the first thing kept on network volumes". The book makes no such claim; it says network storage wins for single-node databases. Hook ("the textbook case for network disks") had the same framing. Both now start from local NVMe being ephemeral, which the book does say.
- ch04-041 [MISLEADING] takeaway "Disaggregation doesn't require a network. PCIe cables can decouple flash from compute." The book defines disaggregation as pooling flash and serving it over the network. Lightning connects the JBOF to a head node by PCIe, but that doesn't make disaggregation network-free. Takeaway replaced.
- ch04-043 [UNSUPPORTED] deeper said Meta puts "latency-critical services, like flash caches" on local flash. The book mentions CacheLib only as an example of caches not needing durability, not as Meta's local-flash use. Removed "like flash caches".
- ch04-056 [MISLEADING, minor] hook said Kubernetes shipped "every storage vendor's plugin" compiled in; the book says only that storage plugins were compiled into Kubernetes. "Every" removed.
- ch04-059 [MISLEADING] mcq answer "The previous pod to be running" and deeper "moves on once a pod is running". A StatefulSet rolling update waits for the pod to be Running *and Ready*, not merely running. Both now say "running and ready". The point is unchanged: it never checks replication.
- ch04-062 [UNSUPPORTED] body said a Kubernetes cluster on your own hardware "has no cloud block storage to claim. Rook fills that hole", and deeper said the December 2018 stable date "shows how long the approach has been in production use". The book says neither. It does not frame Rook as an on-prem tool, and "declared stable" is not a production-use claim. The body now opens with the operational burden of running Ceph, and deeper states the stable date plainly.
